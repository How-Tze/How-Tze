# Hao Xie

Psychology researcher focused on culture, social class, social norms, and social decision-making, with a growing methodological direction in computational and AI-assisted research.

## About

I am a master's student in psychology studying how culture, social class, and social norms shape social judgment and decision-making. Alongside established behavioral-science methods, I am developing computational approaches for organizing evidence, auditing research data, and building reproducible workflows.

I am particularly interested in research infrastructure that turns scattered sources into documented schemas, explicit checks, and usable outputs without losing sight of measurement, context, or confidentiality. My aim is to make complex information-management work easier for researchers to review, collaborate on, and hand off.

AI-assisted development can support implementation, testing, documentation, and iteration, but research requirements, methodological decisions, and final review remain human responsibilities.

## Methods & workflow

**Research methods:** survey design and measurement, meta-analysis, cross-cultural comparison, structural equation modeling, and latent profile analysis.

**Research software:** R for statistical analysis, data manipulation, meta-analysis, and SEM/LPA; SPSS and Mplus for established quantitative workflows.

**Computational tooling:** Python for data processing, file automation, API-supported workflows, and structured research pipelines—often developed with AI assistance and checked through explicit validation, automated tests, and human review. I also use Git, YAML configuration, and reproducible project documentation where they improve transparency.

The emphasis is not technology for its own sake, but research infrastructure that is understandable to domain researchers and can support collaboration, replication, and careful handoff.

## Selected work

### [Opportunity Tracking Pipeline](https://github.com/How-Tze/opportunity-tracking-pipeline)

A configuration-driven Python demonstration for managing structured opportunities. Using entirely synthetic data, it combines validation, duplicate detection, configurable prioritization, deadline and risk logic, automated Excel reporting, and pytest-based testing in a reproducible workflow.

### [Survey Harmonization Toolkit](https://github.com/How-Tze/survey-harmonization-toolkit)

An audit-first Python toolkit for heterogeneous CSV, SPSS, and Stata survey files. It audits metadata, schemas, coverage, missingness, and routing; applies only explicit researcher-reviewed mappings and bounded transformations; blocks ambiguous or unavailable mappings; and produces validated harmonized output with provenance. It does not claim automatic semantic or measurement equivalence.

### AI-assisted research resources

These complementary resources separate durable research-method guidance from fast-changing tools and workflows:

#### [Psych AI Research Handbook](https://how-tze.github.io/psych-ai-research-handbook/)

The stable, versioned core resource for psychologists adopting AI-assisted research workflows. It focuses on durable method principles, practical workflow literacy, and responsible use of AI in research.

#### [Psychology Researcher AI Start Guide](https://How-Tze.github.io/psych-ai-start-guide/)

The dynamic companion to the handbook, covering current products, APIs, coding agents, and changing research workflows. It is maintained separately so time-sensitive guidance can evolve without displacing the handbook's stable core.

## Working principles

I value reproducible workflows, explicit validation, clear publication boundaries, and human review. Computational tools and AI assistance are most useful when they make research reasoning more transparent—not when they obscure it.
